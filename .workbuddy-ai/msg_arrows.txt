Close the seam between an activity-diagram arrow and the shape it leaves.

The user reported that the arrows in Figures 4.5, 4.7 and 4.9 were "not connected
to the block". Measuring the source PNG showed they were: on the evaluation
figure the row through the first decision carries one continuous run from the
diamond's vertex at x=776 to the refusal box at x=1141, and a 5x crop showed a
solid junction.

The fault is at print size, not in the geometry. A connector that begins exactly
on a 2 px outline forms a butt join; a 1800 px figure placed at 16 cm is scaled
to about a quarter, so that join collapses to sub-pixel and reads as a gap.

`actkit.Diagram.connect()` now takes `into=`, which extends the line that many
points past its start so it overlaps the shape. Every refusal branch and every
vertical connector in the five activity diagrams passes into=6. All six
diagrams regenerated and checked at 5x magnification.

Getting the images into the document took a second approach. The chapter had
been rebuilt by the user with their own Mermaid sequence renders and with
Database Design and Interface Design removed, so the embedded activity diagrams
were stale and no file on disk matched them by hash. Position identifies an
image where content does not: every figure is a drawing paragraph followed by
its caption. replace_docx_images_by_caption.py pairs each blip with the caption
that follows it and rewrites only that media part.

Result: the six activity diagrams replaced, the eight user images untouched,
and the document still 85 paragraphs, 9 tables and 23 pages — the user's
structural edits are exactly as they were. A .bak of the previous file is kept.
